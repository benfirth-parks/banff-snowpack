Banff snowpack

Simulated snowpack structure at three study plots, beside the snow profiles observed there.

EXPERIMENTAL snowpack-structure simulation for expert decision support. Not an avalanche forecast, danger rating or operational guidance.

What this does. For the Goat's Eye, Simpson and Bow Summit study plots, a physical snow model (SNOWPACK) builds the snowpack layer by layer from the weather, hour by hour, from bare ground in September. Pick a date and time to see the simulated layering (grain types, hardness, density, temperature) next to the snow profile observed there, with scores for how well they agree.

Inputs and updates. Measured weather from the Parks Canada stations, filled with reanalysis; the daily NOAA GFS forecast for the next three days. The current season updates every morning, along with nearby public reports from Avalanche Canada's Mountain Information Network. Snow profiles you upload below join the comparison.

      Parts of the system and how to reach them status as of 5 Oct 2026

      • Study-plot simulation (in use): SNOWPACK at Goat's Eye, Simpson and Bow Summit, restarted from each observed pit, with the daily 72 h GFS forecast. This page.
      • Daily update (in use): every day at 12:47 UTC new station weather, GFS runs, ERA5 months, nearby public reports and uploaded profiles are collected, the current season is rebuilt and this site is republished. Problems found in the inputs appear in the yellow banner and under Data status.
      • Pit quality control (in use): uploaded profiles are read and checked for a printed date or site that disagrees with the file, and for a location far from the plot. 39 such pits are kept on screen but no longer steer the simulation or count in its scores.
      • Automated testing (in review): every proposed code change is checked automatically, including tests that run the real SNOWPACK engine.
      • Season changeover (in review): the new season starts on 15 September, and a finished season is completed once its last months of reanalysis weather are published.
      • Agent lab (in development, runs on your own computer): a benchmark where different snowpack-prediction methods (SNOWPACK, persistence of the last pit, weather rules, historical analogues and blends of them) predict pits they have not seen, are scored on the same cases, and the better recipes are evolved and re-tested. Research only; its results do not appear on this site.

      Access

      • This site: banff-snowpack.netlify.app, open, no sign-in.
      • Add a profile: the "Add an observation" form above (PDF, CAAML, SnowPilot XML or a photo); it joins the comparison after the next daily update.
      • Data files: the site's JSON files, starting from data/sites.json (seasons per plot) and data/status.json (input freshness and warnings).
      • Code, history and decisions: the GitHub repository benfirth-parks/banff-snowpack (branch main); decisions are in docs/decisions.md, the daily routine in docs/operations.md.
      • Command line: after bash scripts/setup_env.sh in a copy of the repository, snowagent runs every step (for example snowagent update fetch, snowagent web-build, snowagent obs flagged-pits).
      • Agent lab: once released, on a Mac or Linux computer: pip install -e '.[dev,lab]', snowagent lab init, snowagent lab import, then streamlit run lab_app/Home.py (setup guide in docs/lab/local_setup.md).

      How this works

      Each profile on the left is a SNOWPACK simulation (SLF, version shown under the profile) of a flat, open column at the study plot, started from bare ground on 15 September and driven hour by hour with weather. Nothing from the observed profiles is used by the simulation; they are shown only for comparison.

      • Measured weather (from 2015-16 at Goat's Eye and Simpson, 2016-17 at Bow Summit): Parks Canada FTS stations — air temperature at the plot station, humidity, and the nearest precipitation gauge — moved to the plot elevation; wind and radiation from the ERA5 reanalysis. Earlier seasons are driven by ERA5 alone, adjusted to the stations.
      • GFS forecast (Nov–Apr, 2021-22 onward): the snowpack simulated with measured weather up to the forecast's start (00 UTC = 17:00 MST the day before), then run forward on that day's NOAA GFS forecast only. This is what the model would have predicted that far ahead.
      • Live season (the current one): updated daily with measured weather to the latest hour; until ERA5 is published (about three months later) wind, radiation and gaps come from the first 24 h of each day's GFS run. Every day's 00 UTC GFS forecast is kept as it was issued.
      • Pit updates: from the first midnight (UTC) after each observed profile at the plot, the simulation restarts from that profile: its layers, grain forms and hardness (converted to density), at the observed depth, with the simulated temperatures; the weather then evolves it to the next pit. Tested on the next pit it had not seen (136 pairs, 11 seasons, flagged pits left out), grain forms agreed on 57% of the column instead of 46% without updates, hardness and layer boundaries matched better, and the depth error fell from 12 to 9 cm. Profiles without layers update the depth only. The profile shown at a pit's own time never uses that pit. Choose "without pit updates" to see the free run.
      • Hand hardness uses the BELLAIRE relation (chosen by leave-one-season-out tests against the pits). Layers with the same grain form and similar hardness are merged for display.
      • Scores: snow-depth difference; share of the column where the grain class agrees; mean hand-hardness difference (index steps, F=1 … I=6); layer-boundary F1 (boundaries within 2 cm).

      Known limits: the plot column is flat and open (the plots are forest clearings); there is no wind redistribution; precipitation is the station gauge, corrected by a factor chosen leave-one-season-out (Bow Summit and Simpson ×1.15, Goat's Eye ×0.9); the GFS forecast is used raw (it under-forecast some storms by half). Weak-layer flags are grain-form flags, not stability. Observed profiles are transcribed from the field PDFs or read from exact files; transcribed ones can carry errors.
