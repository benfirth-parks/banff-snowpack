Banff snowpack

Evolving a forecast agent that predicts the snowpack structure later observed in the pits at three study plots.

EXPERIMENTAL snowpack-structure simulation for expert decision support. Not an avalanche forecast, danger rating or operational guidance.

Purpose. This tool's main aim is to evolve a forecast agent that predicts snowpack structure accurately: competing prediction methods are tested on past cases against the pits actually dug, and the best recipes are varied and re-tested until they beat the current model on seasons they were not tuned on. How the lab works is just below.

The current model, below that. For the Goat's Eye, Simpson and Bow Summit study plots, a physical snow model (SNOWPACK) builds the snowpack layer by layer from the weather, hour by hour, from bare ground in September. It is the method the agents have to beat. Pick a date and time to see its simulated layering next to the snow profile observed there, with scores for how well they agree. It updates every morning from the Parks Canada stations, reanalysis and the NOAA GFS forecast, along with nearby public reports; snow profiles you upload join the comparison and the lab's test cases.

The evolving forecast agent primary purpose · in development · research only

The lab asks one question: which method best predicts the snowpack that is later observed in a pit? Several prediction "agents" get exactly the same historical cases, each predicts the layering at a plot for a date it has not seen, and the real pit dug that day scores them. The best recipes are then varied and re-tested, generation after generation, so the method improves by evidence rather than by opinion. It runs on a local computer; until an evolved agent beats SNOWPACK on held-out seasons, the profiles below stay the SNOWPACK simulation.

How a test case works
  • Each case has a cut-off time. An agent sees only what was available by then: weather measured up to the cut-off, the GFS forecast issued by then, and pits already dug and published. The pit it must predict, and everything after the cut-off, is kept apart and never passed to an agent; automatic checks refuse a case if a later record slips in.
  • Two kinds of case, ranked separately: 72-hour forecast cases using the archived GFS forecast (about 80 pits since 2021-22), and next-pit cases that predict the next pit from the previous one with the weather as measured in between (about 200 pits since 2015-16), which tests how well an agent evolves the snowpack.
  • Data so far: 536 usable study-plot pits (Bow Summit 262, Goat's Eye 224, Simpson 50) and about 300,000 hours of quality-checked station weather. Simpson has few pits, so its results are the least certain; the lab shows the number of cases beside every score.
The competing agents
  • SNOWPACK, the model behind this site, entered as the method to beat.
  • Persistence: the last pit carried forward, buried by the new snow. The minimum bar.
  • Weather rules: simple, readable rules that add, bury or flag layers from storms, warm spells, clear cold nights and rain on snow.
  • Analogues: finds past weather spells most like the recent one and uses what the pits showed after them.
  • Hybrid: a weighted blend of the others; this is the recipe that evolves.

Every agent returns the same structured prediction: snow depth with a 10-50-90% range, and for each layer its depth range, grain form, hardness, wetness, the chance it is present and whether it is a layer of concern. No written narrative counts toward the score.

Scoring
  • Snow depth (20%): error of the middle estimate and whether the observed depth falls inside the range.
  • Layer structure (30%): observed and predicted layers matched from the surface down; boundary, thickness and order errors.
  • Critical layers (25%): finding and placing surface hoar, facets, depth hoar and crusts.
  • Honest uncertainty (15%): probabilities that match how often things happen, ranges that are neither too narrow nor uselessly wide.
  • Robustness (10%): steady performance across plots, parts of the season and case types.

The weights are fixed in each run's record, and every component score is kept, not only the total.

How the agent evolves
  • An agent's recipe is a typed list of settings, its "genome": temperature and precipitation adjustments per plot, the rain/snow threshold, wind-loading weight, how fast an old pit loses influence, which components are switched on, the blend weights and how wide its uncertainty ranges are.
  • Each generation makes children by small bounded changes to one parent's settings or by combining blocks of settings from two parents. Children are screened on a few cases, survivors run on all development seasons, and only those that also improve on separate validation seasons are kept.
  • Instead of one champion, the lab keeps the best recipe for each niche: overall, each plot, persistent weak layers, crusts, storms, early, mid and late season, uncertainty and robustness.
  • Every child records its parents, each setting it changed, the data version, the random seed and its scores, so any result can be rerun exactly.
Safeguards
  • Seasons are split whole into development, validation and a sealed test that the evolution loop can never call; the sealed test is opened only by hand at milestones.
  • Evolution changes only the allowed settings. It cannot change data, case cut-offs, scoring, the baselines or its own code.
  • Layers made by the rule-based agents are benchmark entries only. What this site shows stays the SNOWPACK simulation unless a lab method beats it on seasons it was not tuned on.
  • Research and decision support only: never a substitute for field assessment, professional avalanche forecasting or local observations.
Status and access
  • Done, in review: data contracts, loading the pits and station weather, and the local app with a profile and weather explorer.
  • Next: benchmark cases and leakage checks; the agents and scoring with leaderboards; the evolution loop and lineage views; then SNOWPACK settings as evolvable genes.
  • Waiting on decisions: which seasons are sealed for the final test, and how long after a dig a pit counts as available.

      Parts of the system and how to reach them status as of 5 Oct 2026

      • Evolving forecast agent (primary purpose; in development, runs on a local computer): a benchmark where different snowpack-prediction methods (SNOWPACK, persistence of the last pit, weather rules, historical analogues and blends of them) predict pits they have not seen, are scored on the same cases, and the better recipes are evolved and re-tested. Research only; its results do not appear on this site. How it works.
      • Study-plot simulation (in use; the method the agents must beat): SNOWPACK at Goat's Eye, Simpson and Bow Summit, restarted from each observed pit, with the daily 72 h GFS forecast. This page.
      • Daily update (in use): every day at 12:47 UTC new station weather, GFS runs, ERA5 months, nearby public reports and uploaded profiles are collected, the current season is rebuilt and this site is republished. Problems found in the inputs appear in the yellow banner and under Data status.
      • Pit quality control (in use): uploaded profiles are read and checked for a printed date or site that disagrees with the file, and for a location far from the plot. 39 such pits are kept on screen but no longer steer the simulation or count in its scores.
      • Automated testing (in review): every proposed code change is checked automatically, including tests that run the real SNOWPACK engine.
      • Season changeover (in review): the new season starts on 15 September, and a finished season is completed once its last months of reanalysis weather are published.

      Access

      • This site: banff-snowpack.netlify.app, open, no sign-in.
      • Add a profile: the "Add an observation" form above (PDF, CAAML, SnowPilot XML or a photo); it joins the comparison after the next daily update.
      • Data files: the site's JSON files, starting from data/sites.json (seasons per plot) and data/status.json (input freshness and warnings).
      • Code, history and decisions: the GitHub repository benfirth-parks/banff-snowpack (branch main); decisions are in docs/decisions.md, the daily routine in docs/operations.md.
      • Command line: after bash scripts/setup_env.sh in a copy of the repository, snowagent runs every step (for example snowagent update fetch, snowagent web-build, snowagent obs flagged-pits).
      • Agent lab: once released, on a Mac or Linux computer: pip install -e '.[dev,lab]', snowagent lab init, snowagent lab import, then streamlit run lab_app/Home.py (setup guide in docs/lab/local_setup.md).

      How this works

      Each profile on the left is a SNOWPACK simulation (SLF, version shown under the profile) of a flat, open column at the study plot, started from bare ground on 15 September and driven hour by hour with weather. Nothing from the observed profiles is used by the simulation; they are shown only for comparison.

      • Measured weather (from 2015-16 at Goat's Eye and Simpson, 2016-17 at Bow Summit): Parks Canada FTS stations — air temperature at the plot station, humidity, and the nearest precipitation gauge — moved to the plot elevation; wind and radiation from the ERA5 reanalysis. Earlier seasons are driven by ERA5 alone, adjusted to the stations.
      • GFS forecast (Nov–Apr, 2021-22 onward): the snowpack simulated with measured weather up to the forecast's start (00 UTC = 17:00 MST the day before), then run forward on that day's NOAA GFS forecast only. This is what the model would have predicted that far ahead.
      • Live season (the current one): updated daily with measured weather to the latest hour; until ERA5 is published (about three months later) wind, radiation and gaps come from the first 24 h of each day's GFS run. Every day's 00 UTC GFS forecast is kept as it was issued.
      • Pit updates: from the first midnight (UTC) after each observed profile at the plot, the simulation restarts from that profile: its layers, grain forms and hardness (converted to density), at the observed depth, with the simulated temperatures; the weather then evolves it to the next pit. Tested on the next pit it had not seen (136 pairs, 11 seasons, flagged pits left out), grain forms agreed on 57% of the column instead of 46% without updates, hardness and layer boundaries matched better, and the depth error fell from 12 to 9 cm. Profiles without layers update the depth only. The profile shown at a pit's own time never uses that pit. Choose "without pit updates" to see the free run.
      • Hand hardness uses the BELLAIRE relation (chosen by leave-one-season-out tests against the pits). Layers with the same grain form and similar hardness are merged for display.
      • Scores: snow-depth difference; share of the column where the grain class agrees; mean hand-hardness difference (index steps, F=1 … I=6); layer-boundary F1 (boundaries within 2 cm).

      Known limits: the plot column is flat and open (the plots are forest clearings); there is no wind redistribution; precipitation is the station gauge, corrected by a factor chosen leave-one-season-out (Bow Summit and Simpson ×1.15, Goat's Eye ×0.9); the GFS forecast is used raw (it under-forecast some storms by half). Weak-layer flags are grain-form flags, not stability. Observed profiles are transcribed from the field PDFs or read from exact files; transcribed ones can carry errors.
