Banff snowpack

Simulated snowpack structure at four study plots in Banff and Jasper, beside the snow profiles observed there.

EXPERIMENTAL snowpack-structure simulation for expert decision support. Not an avalanche forecast, danger rating or operational guidance.

Purpose. This tool's main aim is to evolve a forecast agent that predicts snowpack structure accurately: competing prediction methods are tested on past cases against the pits actually dug, and the best recipes are varied and re-tested until they beat standard SNOWPACK on winters they were not tuned on. How the lab works is just below.

What this does. For four study plots, Goat's Eye, Simpson and Bow Summit in Banff and Parker Ridge in Jasper, snow models build the snowpack layer by layer from the weather, hour by hour. Pick a plot, a date and a time to see the simulated layering (grain types, hardness, density, temperature) next to the snow profile observed there, with scores for how well they agree. Side by side, at the top, shows the site's main model, the best evolved SNOWPACK agent and the observed pit together, and the layers of each model through the season. Below it you can pick any one model and look at it in detail.

The models. Standard SNOWPACK (the Swiss physical snow model, run from bare ground in September) is the reference. The Snowpack Agent Lab evolves challengers to it and scores every one against past pits it was not trained on; the site shows the lab's current main model first (named below) and, when one has been sent to the site, an evolved SNOWPACK agent, which is SNOWPACK with settings the lab tuned. None of them is validated for operational use.

Main model. The layers shown first now come from an evolved model, a snowpack model the Snowpack Agent Lab evolved and tested against past pits (the lab's own column model, no SNOWPACK). Each run starts from the latest pit at the plot and follows the weather since. On winters it never saw it scored about level with standard SNOWPACK: better on facets and depth hoar, worse on crusts, spring snow and the next pit. Standard SNOWPACK stays one choice away under Weather input, and its forecasts are the only forecasts for now.

Inputs and updates. Measured weather from the Parks Canada stations (at Parker Ridge, the Big Bend station 2.9 km away), filled with reanalysis; the daily forecast for the next three days (Environment Canada's HRDPS 2.5 km for two days, then NOAA GFS; from 19 November 2026, earlier forecasts are GFS alone). The current season updates every morning, along with nearby public reports from Avalanche Canada's Mountain Information Network. Snow profiles you upload below join the comparison.

The evolving forecast agent primary purpose · research only · status 22 Nov 2026

The lab asks one question: which method best predicts the snowpack that is later observed in a pit? Every prediction "agent" gets exactly the same historical cases, predicts the layering at a plot for a time it has not seen, and the real pit dug then scores it. The best recipes are varied and re-tested, round after round, so the method improves by evidence rather than by opinion. Training runs on a local computer and in the cloud.

How a test case works
  • Each case has a cut-off time. An agent sees only what was available by then: weather measured up to the cut-off, the forecast issued by then, and pits already dug and published. The pit it must predict, and everything after the cut-off, is kept apart; automatic checks refuse a case if a later record slips in.
  • Two kinds of case: 72-hour forecasts on the archived forecast weather, and next-pit cases that predict the next pit from the previous one with the weather measured in between.
  • Data: about 945 study-plot pits in Banff over 28 winters (Bow Summit, Goat's Eye, Simpson), and 227 at Parker Ridge in Jasper, which is scored but never trained on.
The competing agents
  • Standard SNOWPACK, plain and in its best standard form (snow-depth sensor, new-snow check, snowfall fix): the method to beat.
  • Evolved SNOWPACK agents: SNOWPACK with settings the lab tunes (snowfall, rain/snow threshold, wind, new-snow density, facet and depth-hoar growth and more).
  • The lab's own models, no SNOWPACK: a column model that grows facets, depth hoar, crusts and rounding from the weather and the last pit, and its variants (process switches, story, recipe and scorecard forms). The process-switches winner, the site's main model, scored about level with standard SNOWPACK on winters it never saw: better on facets and depth hoar, worse on crusts, spring snow and the next pit.

Every agent returns the same structured prediction: snow depth with a range, and for each layer its depth, grain form, hardness, wetness, the chance it is present and whether it is a weak layer. No written narrative counts toward the score.

Scoring
  • Snow depth (10%), layer structure (35%), weak layers: finding and placing facets, depth hoar, surface hoar and crusts without false alarms (30%), honest uncertainty (15%), and steady performance across plots, parts of the season and case types (10%).
  • The weights are fixed in each run's record, and every component score is kept, not only the total.
Safeguards against fooling ourselves
  • Whole winters are split: training winters, locked winters (2023-24 to 2025-26) that training never sees, and a blind test on this winter's pits with agents frozen before the pits exist.
  • A promotion check retrains with whole winters held out, one at a time, and the evolved agent must beat standard SNOWPACK on each. Bobby Bada Bing, an evolved SNOWPACK agent, passed it in November 2026 by a small margin.
  • Evolution changes only the allowed settings; it cannot change data, cut-offs, scoring or its own code.
  • Research and decision support only: never a substitute for field assessment, professional avalanche forecasting or local observations.

      Parts of the system and how to reach them status as of 22 Nov 2026

      • Snowpack Agent Lab (primary purpose; runs on a local computer and in the cloud): evolves and tests the agents. How it works.
      • Study-plot simulations (this page): the main model, standard SNOWPACK and any experimental evolved agents at Goat's Eye, Simpson, Bow Summit and Parker Ridge, with the daily 72 h forecast for standard SNOWPACK.
      • Daily update: every morning (about 7 AM Alberta time) new station weather, forecast runs, reanalysis months, nearby public reports and uploaded profiles are collected, the current season is rebuilt and this site is republished. Problems found in the inputs appear in the yellow banner and under Data status.
      • Pit quality control: uploaded profiles are checked for a printed date or site that disagrees with the file and for a location far from the plot; such pits stay on screen but do not steer the simulation or count in its scores.
      • Blind test: frozen agents forecast this winter's pits before they are dug; results build up over the season.

      Access

      • Add a profile: the "Add an observation" form above (PDF, CAAML, SnowPilot XML or a photo); it joins the comparison after the next daily update.
      • Data files: the site's JSON files, starting from data/sites.json (seasons per plot) and data/status.json (input freshness and warnings).
      • Code, history and decisions: the GitHub repository benfirth-parks/banff-snowpack (branch main); decisions are in docs/decisions.md, the daily routine in docs/operations.md.
      • Agent lab: on a Mac or Linux computer, bash scripts/setup_env.sh in a copy of the repository, then snowagent lab app (guide in docs/lab/run_locally.md).

      How this works

      Every simulated profile is a model of a flat, open column at the study plot, driven hour by hour with weather. Three kinds of model are shown. Standard SNOWPACK (SLF, version shown under the profile) starts from bare ground on 15 September; it is the reference every other model is measured against. The main model is the Snowpack Agent Lab's evolved column model (no SNOWPACK inside): it restarts from the latest pit at the plot and follows the weather from there, so it uses the pits; it scored about level with standard SNOWPACK on winters it never saw. An evolved SNOWPACK agent is SNOWPACK with settings the lab tuned against past pits; it runs only in the current season, from measured weather, and is marked experimental. Standard SNOWPACK uses no observed profile except through the pit updates described below.

      The plots. Goat's Eye, Simpson and Bow Summit (Banff) have station weather from 2015-16 (Bow Summit 2016-17) and pits from many winters. Parker Ridge (Jasper, 2010 m, a plot in the trees) was added in November 2026: its weather is the Big Bend station (2125 m, 2.9 km away, moved to the plot's height) from November 2018, and before that the ERA5 reanalysis alone, uncorrected, for the winters with pits (2001-02 to 2013-14). Nothing in the models was tuned to Parker Ridge, so it is a test of the Banff models somewhere they have never been. Big Bend's temperatures arrived without their minus sign from November 2021 to June 2023; those hours are left out and the reanalysis fills them.

      • Measured weather (from 2015-16 at Goat's Eye and Simpson, 2016-17 at Bow Summit, 2018-19 at Parker Ridge): Parks Canada FTS stations — air temperature at the plot station, humidity, and the nearest precipitation gauge — moved to the plot elevation; wind and radiation from the ERA5 reanalysis. Earlier seasons are driven by ERA5 alone, adjusted to the stations in Banff (not at Parker Ridge).
      • Forecast (Nov–Apr, 2021-22 onward; at Parker Ridge from the 2026-27 season): the snowpack simulated with measured weather up to the forecast's start (00 UTC = 17:00 MST the day before), then run forward on that day's forecast only: NOAA GFS, and from 19 November 2026 Environment Canada's HRDPS (2.5 km) for the first 48 h followed by GFS. This is what the model would have predicted that far ahead.
      • Live season (the current one): updated daily with measured weather to the latest hour; until ERA5 is published (about three months later) wind, radiation and gaps come from the first 24 h of each day's GFS run. Every day's 00 UTC forecast is kept as it was issued.
      • Pit updates: from the first midnight (UTC) after each observed profile at the plot, the simulation restarts from that profile: its layers, grain forms and hardness (converted to density), at the observed depth, with the simulated temperatures; the weather then evolves it to the next pit. Tested on the next pit it had not seen (136 pairs, 11 seasons, flagged pits left out), grain forms agreed on 57% of the column instead of 46% without updates, hardness and layer boundaries matched better, and the depth error fell from 12 to 9 cm. Profiles without layers update the depth only. The profile shown at a pit's own time never uses that pit. Choose "without pit updates" to see the free run.
      • Snowfall from the snow-depth sensor (from October 2026): where the plot's snow-depth sensor has readings, new snow comes from the rise in measured depth instead of the precipitation gauge, up to midnight (UTC) of the last reading's day; after that, and in forecasts, it comes from precipitation. Tested on 340 pits from 2015 onward it scored better than the gauge-driven model in 9 of 11 winters, with the depth error falling from 10 to 8 cm. Choose "Previous standard: snowfall from precipitation" to see the model as it was before.
      • New-snow check (from October 2026): the model forms facets, depth hoar and surface hoar in snow that fell since the last pit far more often than observers find them there. In the main forecast these weak-layer-flag layers (higher than 90% of the last pit's snow depth) are drawn faded with a dashed outline and marked unlikely; they are not removed. Tested on 945 pits from 28 winters, treating them as unlikely cut false weak layers by almost half (785 fewer) while missing 28 of about 1000 real ones. A nowcast uses a pit only after that pit's update, a forecast any pit before it was issued.
      • Hand hardness uses the BELLAIRE relation (chosen by leave-one-season-out tests against the pits). Layers with the same grain form and similar hardness are merged for display.
      • Scores: snow-depth difference; share of the column where the grain class agrees; mean hand-hardness difference (index steps, F=1 … I=6); layer-boundary F1 (boundaries within 2 cm).

      Known limits: the plot column is flat and open (the plots are forest clearings); there is no wind redistribution; precipitation is the station gauge, corrected by a factor chosen leave-one-season-out (Bow Summit and Simpson ×1.15, Goat's Eye ×0.9; Parker Ridge uncorrected, ×1); the GFS forecast is used raw (it under-forecast some storms by half). Weak-layer flags are grain-form flags, not stability. Observed profiles are transcribed from the field PDFs or read from exact files; transcribed ones can carry errors.
