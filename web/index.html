How this works

Each profile on the left is a SNOWPACK simulation (SLF, version shown under the profile) of a flat, open column at the study plot, started from bare ground on 15 September and driven hour by hour with weather. Nothing from the observed profiles is used by the simulation; they are shown only for comparison.

  • Measured weather (from 2015-16 at Goat's Eye and Simpson, 2016-17 at Bow Summit): Parks Canada FTS stations — air temperature at the plot station, humidity, and the nearest precipitation gauge — moved to the plot elevation; wind and radiation from the ERA5 reanalysis. Earlier seasons are driven by ERA5 alone, adjusted to the stations.
  • GFS forecast (Nov–Apr, 2021-22 onward): the snowpack simulated with measured weather up to the forecast's start (00 UTC = 17:00 MST the day before), then run forward on that day's NOAA GFS forecast only. This is what the model would have predicted that far ahead.
  • Hand hardness uses the BELLAIRE relation (chosen by leave-one-season-out tests against the pits). Layers with the same grain form and similar hardness are merged for display.
  • Scores: snow-depth difference; share of the column where the grain class agrees; mean hand-hardness difference (index steps, F=1 … I=6); layer-boundary F1 (boundaries within 2 cm).

Known limits: the plot column is flat and open (the plots are forest clearings); there is no wind redistribution; precipitation is the station gauge, uncorrected except at Bow Summit and Simpson (×1.15); the GFS forecast is used raw (it under-forecast some storms by half). Weak-layer flags are grain-form flags, not stability. Observed profiles are transcribed from the field PDFs or read from exact files; transcribed ones can carry errors.
